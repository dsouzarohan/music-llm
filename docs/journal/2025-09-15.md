### Here are some resources that talk about these practices (must read):

- **GPT-3 Paper (Brown et al., 2020)**
    Mentions that training is done by sampling **random contiguous spans** of tokens from documents.
    👉 [Language Models are Few-Shot Learners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)
- **The Pile (Gao et al., 2020)**
    Large dataset paper; describes careful splitting and weighting of different corpora.
    👉 [The Pile: An 800GB Dataset of Diverse Text for Language Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027)
- **Megatron-LM (Shoeybi et al., 2019)**
    Describes efficient dataloaders that split long documents into random fixed-length chunks on the fly.
    👉 [Megatron-LM: Training Multi-Billion Parameter Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)
- **PaLM (Chowdhery et al., 2022)**
    Scaling paper; describes corpus curation, deduplication, and random span sampling.
    👉 [PaLM: Scaling Language Models with Pathways](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)
- **Music-specific**:
    - *“PopMAG: Pop Music Accompaniment Generation” (Ren et al., 2020)* → uses random segment sampling of music.
    - *“MuseNet” (OpenAI blog, 2019)* → mentions random contiguous chunks from MIDI.
