# 2025-11-30

Finally was able to run this on A100, well not the training but the preparation. Because I faced yet another CUDA OOM error while training. Even the A100’s 80GB VRAM wasn’t enough

Did some research and found out that it’s most likely due to the size of the attention matrix. The error with 100M parameters on an A100 is almost certainly caused by the **Attention Matrix** size during training, not the model parameters themselves.

Even though we implemented the "efficient" skewing trick for relative attention (which saves memory on the *embeddings*), the manual implementation of attention still requires PyTorch to store a massive $`L \times L`$ score matrix for every single head, layer, and batch item to calculate gradients.

**The Math of the Crash:**<br>• **Batch:** 64<br>• **Heads:** 12<br>• **Context:** 2048<br>• **Matrix Size:** $`64 \times 12 \times 2048 \times 2048 \times 2 \text{ bytes (float16)} \approx \mathbf{6.4 \text{ GB}}`$<br>• **Total:** With 12 layers, storing these matrices for backpropagation requires **\~77 GB** of VRAM just for attention scores. Add in the Feed-Forward layers, model weights, and optimizer states, and you instantly blow past 80GB.

### Next steps

- We need to implement something called gradient accumulation that is **a technique that simulates a larger batch size by accumulating gradients over multiple smaller batches before updating the model's weights**
- With this, the physical batch size will be around 8 to 16 to fit in memory, but the effective batch size at 64 to maintain training stability. We do this by accumulating gradients over multiple small steps before updating the model.
